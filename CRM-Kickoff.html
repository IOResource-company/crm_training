Supply. Configure. Support.01
Purpose

What the CRM is for

One record of the pursuit

The account, the opportunity, the stage and the next action. Held in a shared system rather than in one person’s mailbox.

Follow-up driven by fields

An opportunity with a next step and a date appears on the follow-up views. Without them it appears on none.

Loss reasons recorded

The CRM is the only system that stores why a deal was lost. Nothing else captures it.

Sales has not been live on the CRM before now. Nothing in this deck refers to past work.

Supply. Configure. Support.02
Systems

Four systems, and how they join

sales@ioresource.com

Shared mailbox. Customer email in and out. Synced to the CRM at subject, participants and date. Bodies and attachments are not stored.

Shared files

The sales-meetings folder. Weekly meeting minutes are ingested as Tasks. The rep pipeline files are swept every Tuesday at 15:00.

The CRM

Companies, contacts, opportunities, cases, tasks and notes. Everything up to the order.

Intact

Orders, stock, delivery and invoicing. System of record for anything ordered. Where the two disagree, Intact is correct.

Your sheet wins on

amount · probability · next step

The CRM wins on

stage · close date

Join points. The document number appears in the email subject, in Quote / PO Ref on the opportunity, and on the Intact document. Account code joins the company record to Intact.

The Tuesday sweep never marks a deal Won and never deletes anything — rows it cannot reconcile become a single review task. Meeting actions arrive as Tasks titled Sales mtg W32 #14; that number is permanent, so an unfinished action carries into the next week keeping it. Three carries and it is escalated: kill it or commit a date.

Supply. Configure. Support.03
Adding by email

Forward to sales@ and type one word Open

First line of the forward

Sales

Four forms

  • Sales — the engine selects the action
  • Sales lead — create an opportunity
  • Sales task — create a task
  • Sales note — file a note on the matched record

What happens

  • Reads the full body and compares it against all open opportunities, cases and companies
  • Acts above a 0.8 confidence threshold; below it, raises a task titled Review: Sales Pipe mail
  • Writes a note on every record it touches, listed in the next Pulse under Actions taken

Sales and Ops are separated by the sender. Customer email arriving at sales@ creates a Case for Customer Operations. A forward from an @ioresource.com address beginning with Sales is routed to the pipeline and excluded from case creation.

Open. The one-word trigger reads the body and is agreed but not yet shipped. Until it is, the working form is (Sales Pipe mail) anywhere in the subject line.

Supply. Configure. Support.04
Record types

Company and End Customer

Company

The trade account we invoice — reseller, system integrator, VAR or IT team. We are trade-only, so this is rarely the site using the hardware.

End Customer

The business the hardware is deployed to.

Worked example. A reseller supplies a grocery chain. Company is the reseller. End Customer is the grocery chain.

With both populated, competing resellers pursuing the same site roll up in a single view. Entered the wrong way round, the records have to be rebuilt to correct it.

Supply. Configure. Support.05
Pipeline

The six stages

Newreceived
Screeningqualifying
Meetingengaged
Proposalquote issued
Customerwon
Closed Lostlost
  • Customer is the won stage. There is no separate Closed Won. The name describes the outcome of that one deal, not the status of the account.
  • Stage belongs to the opportunity, not the account. A company we have sold to for years can carry a Closed Lost deal. That is a lost deal, not a lost customer. The relationship is held separately in Account Status and Account Tier on the company record, and closing a deal lost does not touch either.
  • Closed Lost requires a lost reason. It is a single click and there is no other record of it.
  • A won deal left at Proposal counts in open pipeline, carries into the forecast, and stays on the quote follow-up views.
  • A closed-lost deal is reopened by moving its stage. Do not create a second opportunity.
Supply. Configure. Support.06
Fields

The six fields that carry the reporting

Sales RepOn the company. Rep-scoped views and the Pulse read this field. An account with it blank appears on no rep’s list.
StageWhere the opportunity is. Moved on the day it changes.
Amount and basisQuoted, then ordered. An opportunity with no amount is excluded from the pipeline total.
Close dateWhen the order is expected to land. If it slips, move the date and record why in the next step.
Next step and dateThe action and when it is due. Both are required for the deal to appear on a follow-up view.
Quote / PO RefExpected from Proposal onward. Joins the opportunity to the Intact document.

Supply. Configure. Support.08
Closing

Closing won and closing lost

Purchase order received

  • Stage to Customer, same day
  • Amount to the quantity ordered
  • Close date to the date it landed
  • Quote / PO Ref entered
  • Note recording what was ordered and the destination

Lost

  • Stage to Closed Lost
  • Lost reason set — never blank
  • Price · Lead Time · Lost to Competitor · No Budget · No Decision · Stalled · Other
  • Competitor fields set where a competitor won it

Lost reasons are read as a set. Lead Time losses go to supplier discussions; Price losses go to pricing. A blank reason contributes to neither and cannot be reconstructed later.

Supply. Configure. Support.09
Daily routine

The Sales Pulse

One email each weekday morning, around 08:10. Your name is in the subject and every figure is narrowed to the accounts where you are the Sales Rep.

What arrives

  • A pipeline read: a short health summary, then three to five deals to touch today, a maintain list and up to three watch items
  • Your deal rows, with quote follow-ups cross-posted from the Ops side
  • Meeting actions, grouped by owner
  • An Actions taken section recording what the automation and the buttons did, and who clicked

The buttons write to the CRM

  • Won · Lost · Push +3d / +1m / +3m · Comment · Assign · Open deal
  • Meeting actions: Done · Carry · Answer
  • Won sets stage Customer at 100%, closing today. Lost forces a reason from the list. Both stop any cadence.
  • Monday adds Apply on the timing proposals
Never reply to a Pulse to comment

Mail sync keeps only the unquoted top of a reply. Anything typed beside the quoted rows is discarded — that has already cost two meetings’ worth of comments. Use the Comment button, which files it as a note on the record.

Buttons open a confirm page and the write happens when you confirm, because Outlook pre-fetches every link in an email. Signed links expire after 21 days. Monday’s edition adds the data check, with a pipeline file attached and a per-stage reconciliation. Customer Operations receive their own Pulse twice daily.

Data quality

Search before creating a company

The CRM holds the name as it was entered, not as printed on the purchase order.

Returns nothing

McLernon Computers Limited

Returns two records

mclern

  • Test three variants: plural, capitalisation, spacing. Search the customer’s email domain as well.
  • Two records for one customer: work in the one holding the open opportunities, and flag the other.
  • On a new record, set Sales Rep and Account code before moving on.

Duplicate pairs already exist in the loaded data. They arrived with the load rather than from anybody’s entry, but do not add to them — a split account reports as two small ones.

Sign-off

What we are committing to from this week

  • Every opportunity touched leaves with a next step and a next step date.
  • Stage moves the day the deal moves, including on a win.
  • Search before creating a company. Short fragment, then the email domain.
  • Anything material from a call or an email is entered as a note.
  • Lost reason set on every loss.
  • The Sales Pulse is worked each morning — buttons, not replies.
