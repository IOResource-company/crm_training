Sales Kick-Off01
Why we are doing this

Nothing here is about admin.
It is about not losing work.

One place for the pursuit

Who we are chasing, what stage it is at, and what happens next — visible to more than one person.

Nothing quietly dropped

A deal with a next step and a date turns up on a list. A deal without one goes quiet by default rather than by decision.

Reasons, not just results

Why we lose is the cheapest market research we get, and the CRM is the only place it is ever recorded.

Nobody has got any of this wrong yet — we have not been live. That is exactly why we are covering it now rather than in six months.

Sales Kick-Off02
The one-minute model

Two systems. One join.

The CRM holds the pursuit

The relationship, the opportunity, the next step, the reason we won or lost. Everything before the order.

Intact holds the order

Orders, stock, delivery, invoicing. It is the system of record. When the two disagree about what was ordered, Intact wins.

The join: Quote / PO Ref on the deal, Account code on the company. Fill those and the two systems can be reconciled against each other. Leave them blank and they cannot.

So: do not go looking for a delivery date or an invoice in the CRM. It is not there, and it is not meant to be.

Sales Kick-Off03
The distinction that matters most

Company is who we invoice.
End Customer is who the kit is for.

Company

The trade account — the reseller, integrator, VAR or IT team we raise the invoice to. We are trade-only, so this is almost never the site using the hardware.

End Customer

The business the deployment is actually for. Fill this and you can see every reseller chasing the same site in one view.

Get these the wrong way round and it is the most awkward thing in the CRM to unpick later. If a reseller is supplying a grocery chain, the reseller goes in Company and the chain goes in End Customer — not the other way round, and not both in one field.

The stage ladder

Six stages. Two of them are endings.

Proposalquote is out

Customer means won. There is no separate Closed Won stage. A won deal left sitting at Proposal inflates open pipeline, distorts the forecast, and keeps appearing on quote follow-up lists — so somebody chases a customer who has already bought.

The stage is a fact about the deal, not a feeling about it.

Sales Kick-Off05
The fields that carry the weight

Six of them. That is the whole ask.

Sales RepOn the company. Ownership hangs off this — an unowned account shows in nobody’s Pulse, so a new customer is invisible until somebody claims it.
StageWhere the deal actually is. Move it the day it moves.
Amount + basisWhat was quoted, then what was ordered. A deal with no amount is simply absent from the pipeline total.
Close dateWhen the order will realistically land — not when you would like it to. Slipped? Move it and say why in the next step.
Next step + dateThe action and when it is due. Both, or the deal appears on no list at all.
Quote / PO RefExpected once a quotation is out. This is the tie back to Intact.
Sales Kick-Off06
The one habit worth arguing about

Write the next step for a stranger.

Useless to anyone else

chase
follow up
waiting on customer
ongoing 2026

Somebody could act on this

Ring Fiachra to confirm DT50S quantities on quotation 2119 — Thursday

Name the person, the thing, the document and the date. The test: if a colleague picked the deal up cold tomorrow, could they do it without ringing you?

And if the honest answer is that there is no next step, the deal is not open. Close it with a reason.

Sales Kick-Off07
Closing, both ways

Five edits for a win.
One click for a loss.

The PO lands

  • Stage to Customer, the same day
  • Amount to what was actually ordered
  • Close date to the day it landed
  • Quote / PO Ref filled
  • One note: what shipped, and where to

They buy elsewhere

  • Stage to Closed Lost
  • Lost reason — never blank
  • Price · Lead Time · Competitor · No Budget · No Decision · Stalled
  • If a competitor won it, name them
  • They come back? Reopen the same deal

Lead Time losses are a supplier conversation. Price losses are a margin conversation. A blank reason is neither, and it cannot be reconstructed later. Closing a loss is not an admission — it is the raw material for win rate.

Sales Kick-Off08
Thirty seconds that saves an afternoon

Search short before you create anything.

The CRM holds the name somebody typed, not the name on the letterhead. A purchase order headed McLernon Computers Limited can come back empty if you search the full string.

Comes back empty

McLernon Computers Limited

Finds both records

mclern

Three shapes to watch for: the plural, the capital letter, the straight repeat. Search a short fragment and the customer’s email domain, and only create when it is genuinely absent. If you find twins, work the one holding the open deals and flag the other.

Sales Kick-Off09
What we are asking this week

Four things, and they are all small.

  • Every deal you touch leaves with a next step and a date.
  • Stage moves the day the deal moves — especially on a win.
  • Search before you create a company. Short fragment, then the email domain.
  • Anything you are unsure about, put it in a note. A note nobody needed costs nothing; a decision that lived only in one inbox costs a customer.

The full guide is online with how-tos, worked examples and a short quiz, and a five-minute training email lands each working day. If something in the CRM does not match the guide, say so — that has already caught four things this week.
